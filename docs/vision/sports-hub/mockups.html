Sports Hub: three layouts to pick from

Mock-ups only, nothing built. Every option keeps the fixed decisions from SPORTS_HUB.md §9: category rows plus a hero, rectangular game cards (Away over Home with logos, local start time, network, LIVE badge), scores shown by default, every game listed even when no stream matched, Philadelphia as the demo "My teams" row, the leagues NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, UFC and boxing, and OK on a card opens a Teleport-style modal that lists matched streams, best match first. Option A shows the modal open. Option B shows the score setting (Shown, Blurred, Blank).

Drawn in Crest + Surf: Barlow Condensed figures, square cards, a solid red focus block, red only for focus, LIVE and progress, and the crescent on the video edge and the modal. The Surf grain is used only behind the modal, which counts as a calm Teleport surface. Team colours stay inside the logo circles. Artboards are 1920×1080 px (960×540 dp), scaled to fit. The match data is placeholder demo data, not real fixtures.

Where the ideas came from

A short look at how the big streaming apps lay out live sports. Reported means from their help pages or press coverage. Prior knowledge means general knowledge of the app that I did not check this session.

Apple TV appReported: a Settings toggle, "Show Sports Scores", hides live scores across the app, and "Manage My Sports" lets you follow teams; followed teams' live games surface on Home (Apple TV settings, Follow sports teams). MLS Season Pass got its own sidebar entry in the 2023 redesign (Apple Newsroom). Prior knowledge: a big live-game hero over rows of score cards.
Used in: A (hero over rows, My teams first), the score setting in B, C's team-first framing.
PeacockReported: the Olympics hub put a spotlight of the biggest live events at the top, a "Browse by Sport" rail, and connected navigation across the top for jumping between sport hubs. "Live in Browse" starts playback on the browse screen, and "Key Plays" catches you up on a game in progress (SVG, StreamTV Insider, SVG on NBA).
Used in: A's spotlight hero, B's live preview and league chips, C's league tiles.
HBO MaxReported: Sports is its own section with live games, upcoming games and recent highlights, plus a Sports Schedule covering the next two weeks; on TVs the menu is a left rail (HBO Max help, TechRadar).
Used in: B's day picker, C's rows grouped by time (Live now, Later tonight, Tomorrow).
Stremio / Nuvio, TivraTV, TivEPGThe user's own reference (§9.6): one horizontal row per category. From the brief's prior art: TivEPG's live scoreboard rail, TivraTV's "OK previews, OK again goes fullscreen" and its sources sheet, and GloIPTV's reminders.
Used in: every option's rows; B's ticker and preview flow; C's reminder.
RedSurf itselfTeleport's panel and crescent for the sources modal; Live TV's hero band and shared player for B's preview; the exit toast's inverse style for hints.

A · Marquee Apple TV / Peacock spotlight + Stremio rows

A large hero that follows focus, with rows of big game cards below. The focused card's game fills the top: big logos, the score, the clock, and a muted preview of the best stream on the right, behind the crescent cut. Under it sit one row per category: My teams (Philadelphia) first, then Live now, then one row per league. It is the closest match to the user's Stremio/Nuvio picture and to the Apple TV and Peacock pattern: you browse big cards and the hero tells you what you're on. OK opens the sources modal (second frame), and OK in the modal plays.

Strengths

  • Most like the reference apps, so it feels familiar at once.
  • Big cards and a big score read well from across the room.
  • The hero preview reuses the shared player the Live TV hero already has.

Trade-offs

  • Only about 1.5 rows on screen. On a busy Sunday, scanning every NFL game takes many presses.
  • Horizontal rows of large cards are new UI with their own frame-time budget, and the hero redraws on every focus move.
  • No date control: a later day needs a row or a separate screen.
Focus: the Eagles card in My teams, a solid red block. The hero shows that game; the preview plays muted after a 700 ms settle. Union @ home has no matched stream, so it says "Not in your playlists" (§9.3). UP/DOWN moves between rows, LEFT/RIGHT moves along a row, Back goes to the nav strip.
OK on the card → the sources modal. Teleport's panel and crescent on the Surf backdrop. Streams are listed best match first, each with its playlist, the reason it matched and a confidence label: Best = EPG plus both team names, Likely = the network or a simulcast partner, Possible = a channel-name hit only. Home-market affiliates and RSNs come from §9.7's hint. OK plays the row; Back closes and returns focus to the same card.

B · Live Desk Peacock live-in-browse + Max schedule + TivEPG ticker

A working sports desk. A slim live scoreboard ticker runs under the nav. Below it, a compact hero: the real preview video on the left, as in Live TV, with the focused game's details and its best source on the right. Then a chip bar to filter by league (Peacock's connected navigation) and by day (Max's two-week schedule), and three rows of smaller cards. Rows show more games at once, the preview really plays (OK previews, OK again goes fullscreen, TivraTV's flow), and the chips cut a busy day down to one league in one press.

Strengths

  • Densest: about 3 rows and 15 cards on screen, and the ticker shows every live score without moving.
  • Same hero and player as Live TV, so it feels like one app and shares code.
  • The day chips answer "what's on Saturday" without a new screen.

Trade-offs

  • The busiest screen. The ticker plus the chips plus the preview is a lot of moving parts on a weak GPU, and the ticker should not animate while video plays.
  • Smaller cards make the logos and scores harder to read from the sofa.
  • Chips add a second focus row, so focus needs care when you return to the hub (FOCUS_MODEL rule).
Focus: the 76ers card in the Philadelphia row, and the preview plays that game. The ticker and the hero follow the score setting too. The NBA chip's red count is the number of live games.
The score setting (§9.2), which applies to every option: Settings → Sports → "Scores on game cards". It cycles Shown (the default), Blurred (the score is blurred until you open the game) and Blank (a dash, nothing to read). The same setting covers the hero, the ticker and the modal header.

C · Game Day Apple "My Sports" team-first + Max time-grouped schedule

Philadelphia first. The hero is a fixed team spotlight, not a hero that follows focus: the user's number-one team (Eagles), live or next, with "Where to watch" as focusable source rows right in the hero, the last result, and a reminder for the next game. Below, rows are grouped by time, not by league: My teams, Live now, Later tonight, Tomorrow. Each card carries its league tag and its best source already filled in, and a row of league tiles at the bottom opens a full schedule for each league. This is the brief's option C used as the whole screen: open the hub and the Eagles game is one press away.

Strengths

  • The fastest route to the family's own games: the spotlight's top source is the first focus.
  • Grouping by time answers "what's on now" across every league in one row.
  • The hero never redraws on focus moves, so it is cheaper than A and B.

Trade-offs

  • Weaker for browsing one league: NFL games are spread across rows until you open the NFL tile.
  • The spotlight takes room even on days with no Philadelphia game. It would shrink to "Next: Sun 1:00 PM".
  • Two kinds of focus target in the hero (source rows and buttons) plus cards below.
Focus: the Phillies card in My teams. Its best source ("FOX 29 Philadelphia · Magnum") sits on the card, OK opens the same sources modal as A, and holding OK plays the top source at once. UP from the top row enters the spotlight's source rows.
